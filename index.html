Clone the repository from GitHub, then from the project root run:

The app will start at http://localhost:4200. To connect to your team's isolated database schema, use either method:

Set the Tone

Bug-Busting Mindset

Bugs on release day come with the territory. A calm War Room isn't one where nothing broke — it's one that knows how to react. Three pillars carry that.

Pillar 1

Psychological Safety

Nobody debugs well while getting blamed. Name what you see, hand your teammates the context, and decide together what needs to move first — panic and finger-pointing stay outside the room.

Pillar 2

Structured Prioritization

Every ticket gets weighed against user pain and engineering cost before anyone starts fixing. A deliberate call beats a fast guess, and the framework — not the loudest voice — decides what's next.

Pillar 3

Leverage your voice

Own your communication and back your decisions with clear rationale. Make sure your reasoning is visible, not just assumed.

The measure of progress: a new error you haven't hit yet.

What Success Looks Like

Your Objectives

By the end of the workshop, every participant should have practiced these four moves.

  1. Rank every bug on Priority × Effort. Sort the incoming report into P1/P2/P3 with a rough effort estimate. Be ready to defend your ranking with one sentence per ticket.
  2. Choose the approach consciously: workaround or root cause. For each bug you pick up, decide whether to ship a quick mitigation now or dig for the actual root cause. Both are correct answers in the right column of the matrix.
  3. Show your work. Every triage call and every fix comes with the "why" — grounded in user impact and effort, not gut feel. Your teammates should be able to follow your reasoning without asking.
  4. Ask for help early. Teammates first, the guide's Hints and AI Templates second, facilitator third. Being stuck for 20 minutes silently is a bug in the process, not a badge.

Triage & Prioritize

Bug Severity Levels

Not every bug is created equal. Use these severity levels to decide which bugs to tackle first as a team.

Priority × Effort — the quick-triage matrix

Once every ticket has a priority (P1 = critical, P3 = cosmetic) and a rough effort estimate, drop it on the grid. Top-left is your Quick Win — highest impact, lowest cost. Start there.

Low Effort Medium Effort High Effort
P1 Quick Win — do first Do now; assign your best debugger Ship a workaround, schedule the real fix
P2 Slot in this sprint Next sprint Ship a workaround now; split the real fix across sprints
P3 Batch with the next release Backlog Assign an owner and revisit date
Workaround vs. root-cause fix — pick on purpose. A workaround shrinks the blast radius today (a feature flag, a hidden button, a "we know" banner). A root-cause fix removes the bug for good but takes longer to land. On release day, most of the right-column cells above are asking you to ship the workaround now and schedule the real fix — not choose between them.
Critical

Blocker — Feature Completely Broken

No workaround exists. Prevents a primary user flow. Causes severe loss of revenue, data loss, security vulnerabilities, or widespread app crashes.

Example impact: Users can't check out, the cart total is wrong by orders of magnitude, or the app crashes on load.
Major

Behavioral — Badly Frustrating UX

A workaround exists but causes highly frustrating user experience. Frustrated users can still eventually check out, but something important is clearly wrong.

Example impact: The cart doesn't update correctly, a promotional code fails to apply, or a form rejects valid input.
Minor

Cosmetic — Polish & Alignment

Typographical errors or minor alignment issues with negligible impact. The app functions correctly but looks less polished. Users can still shop without friction.

Example impact: A button has the wrong shade of color, a label has a typo, or icons are slightly misaligned.

AI as Your Sidekick

AI Debugging Templates

AI agents can accelerate your debugging — but only if you prompt them well. Know where they shine and where they need a push, then use the three templates below.

AI's strong suits

  • Decoding a cryptic error — paste the surrounding code and one sentence about what you were doing; you'll usually get a real lead.
  • Spotting the mechanical stuff: typos, missing semicolons, swapped argument order. Anything a second read would catch.
  • Drafting unit tests once you've told it the behavior you want. Coverage without the boilerplate.

Where AI needs a nudge

  • It can't see your app. Describe what's on screen and what you expected instead — vague prompts get vague fixes.
  • Don't accept the first suggestion. Make it defend its logic, then read the diff before you click accept.
  • Feed it the framework, file paths, and data shape — otherwise you'll get textbook Angular answers to your very specific bug.

1. Precise Logic Fix

When you've found the exact function or line that's wrong, give the AI full context and ask for a targeted fix.

"Please look at this specific function below and tell me what is causing this bug (e.g., $2010 total). Do not rewrite the entire file, just show the corrected function. [Paste Code]."

2. Narrow Console Error

When you have an error message but aren't sure where it originates, paste the error and ask for likely causes.

"I am getting this error on my console: [Paste Error]. It triggers when I fire this specific method: [Paste Method]. What are the top 3 most likely causes in Angular?"

3. Broad Exploration

When you're stuck and need a starting point, describe the unexpected behavior and let the AI suggest where to look.

"The cart total shows $2,010 instead of $20.10. The relevant files are cart.service.ts and checkout.component.ts. Walk me through the data flow from adding an item to displaying the total, and flag where a string-to-number conversion issue might occur."

Remember: Audit the Output

Stop hallucinations in their tracks. Stress-test the AI's suggestions, push back on assumptions, and force the agent to defend its logic. Did it catch all the edge cases? Own your solution — don't just click "accept."

Your Toolkit

Hints for Success

📝
Document clearly. Write down what you found, how you found it, and your fix. Collaborative notes help the whole team.
🤝
Collaborate often. Share your screen, pair up, and talk through problems. Two perspectives catch more than one.
🃏
Reach for the guide. Stuck? Skim the Bug Guide for repro steps and the AI Templates for prompting patterns. A facilitator is your last resort — not your first.
🔍
Use DevTools. The Console, Network tab, and Elements inspector are your best friends. Check for errors, failed requests, and DOM state.
🏆
Own the solution. Don't just fix it — understand why it broke and explain your reasoning to your teammates.
💡
Advocate with intent. Practice justifying technical decisions and driving team priorities based on your role's perspective.
